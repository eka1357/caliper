# Official participant guide (copied Oct 26 2026)

Source: Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon participant guide on the Cerebral Valley event page. Copied as received; the event page is authoritative if it changes.

## 1. Problem statements

Your project must build towards one of the following problem statements:

- **Build for Your World**: Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that wasn't before.
- **Push the Frontier**: Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show us what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.

Your project must incorporate Meta's models. Use the Muse Spark series, Muse Wonder, Muse Image, Muse Voice Transcribe and SAM 3.1 through Meta Model API, run Meta open weights models Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open, and code faster with Muse Code coding agent.

## 4. Rules

- **Open source**: repositories must be public.
- **Team size**: maximum four; solo participants allowed.
- **Demo requirements**: the demo must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality built during the Hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify original contributions results in immediate disqualification at the judges' sole discretion.
- **New work only**: what you present must have been built during the event, and this must be clear in the submission. (Startups may build on existing products if they clearly identify what is new.)
- **Banned projects**: disqualified if they violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, or use code, data, or assets you do not have the rights to.
- **Sample anti-projects** (avoid): AI that generates and gives medical advice (mental health advisor, skin blemish detector, etc.); basic RAG applications; simple image analyzers; simple "AI for Education" chatbots; AI job application screeners; AI nutrition coaches; personality analyzers; sports analyzers or coaches; anything political or involving war.

## 7. Submission

- Submit on the CV platform by **Nov 4, 9:00 PM EST**. One submission per team; a Representative submits.
- Public repository, accessible video link, everyone who worked on it listed.
- **Three-minute demo video** highlighting the features, code, and functionality built during the Hackathon. Judges use the video, the code, and the live product.

## 8. Judging

- **Stage One (pass/fail)**: the project reasonably fits a theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models, built during the Hackathon.
- **Stage Two**: four equally weighted criteria, each scored 0 to 10:

| Criterion | What judges ask |
| --- | --- |
| Impact | Real-world potential; who benefits and how much; could people actually use it; does it fit a problem statement |
| Use of Meta AI Tooling | How creatively Meta's tooling is used; beyond basic integration; capabilities that surprised even Meta |
| Technical Depth and Execution | Sound, refined engineering; real craft |
| Demo | Working, impressive demo; holds up live; the video is genuinely cool to watch |

- **Stage Three**: the top 5 per track go to a final round where judges watch the video demos live and rank them on the same rubric.
